Voice Lab: Auditory-Perceptual Evaluation

CAPE-V-style digital rating worksheet · PT108 and PT032

Instructions

Practice systematic listening and use the Consensus Auditory-Perceptual Evaluation of Voice (CAPE-V) to describe voice quality. You will rate two recorded samples, compare their perceptual features, and reflect on the rating process.

Before you begin

Download the blank CAPE-V form and the two assigned audio files from Canvas. When listening to the audio samples, use headphones when possible, be in a quiet environment without background noise, and listen at a comfortable listening volume. Review the six CAPE-V parameters from the class lecture: overall severity, roughness, breathiness, strain, pitch, and loudness.

Patient descriptions

Sample 1 (Patient A): 77-year-old man presenting for voice evaluation due to persistent changes in voice quality. He describes his voice as “gravelly” and reports that his wife has difficulty understanding him. His voice changes have been somewhat gradual; he first noticed a change about a year ago, but it is getting worse.

Sample 2 (Patient B): A 52-year-old woman presenting for voice evaluation due to vocal fatigue and vocal effort throughout the day. She is an office manager for a law firm and notices an unreliable voice quality (sometimes her voice is OK, other times it’s not), which causes frustration and disruption at work. Occasionally she experiences throat pain near the level of her larynx, especially after talking a lot.

To begin

  1. Start with Sample 1: Listen to the entire recording all the way through before making any ratings.
  2. Replay the recording and think about which parameter(s) stand out to you the most.
  3. Replay the recording as necessary, rating one parameter at a time by placing a mark on each rating line (or moving the corresponding slider in this digital form). As you rate, consider not just mild–moderate–severe, but where the voice falls within each category (e.g., low end of moderate vs. high end of moderate). Don’t forget to indicate C or I (consistent/intermittent), and use the comment area for pitch and loudness to describe any problems with those two categories if relevant to this case.
  4. Take a look at your ratings and make sure they reflect the prevalent voice parameters you identified to begin with.

Repeat these steps for Sample 2. Complete the reflection questions, then select Print / Save as PDF and upload the completed PDF to Canvas. MI = mildly deviant; MO = moderately deviant; SE = severely deviant. The digital sliders represent relative 0–100 positions, not physical screen millimeters. This teaching worksheet is not the official standardized CAPE-V form.

Clinical Reasoning and Reflection

Answer each question briefly (approximately 2–4 sentences).

Tip: On most computers, choose Print → Destination: Save as PDF. Check the PDF preview to ensure both patient ratings and written responses appear.
